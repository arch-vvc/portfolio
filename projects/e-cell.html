Business crisis simulation

PESU Entrepreneurship Cell, Organising Committee

The problem

Case competitions hand students one polished case and a deck template. Real managers get a crisis they did not pick, with half the facts, and have to decide anyway.

What it is

A Jeopardy style board of 20 corporate crises for teams to pick from, run end to end for 100+ participants. The cases sit on four tiers, from everyday office problems up to the crises that can end a company. Behind the board is a bank of 80+ written cases.

Every case has the same shape: a scenario, the key challenges, a real precedent from a company that lived through something similar, and discussion points that push a team toward a decision.

The board

Tier 4, 100 points

  • Company saving M&A falls through
  • Environmental crisis
  • Threat of a hostile takeover
  • Death of the CEO or co founder with an IPO imminent
  • War breaks out and you cannot support your whole workforce

Tier 3, 200 points

  • Big product launch delayed by three years
  • Expansion into a new country fails

Tier 2, 300 points

Tier 1, 400 points

  • Payment page breakdown
  • Harassment at a company retreat
  • New employees find it hard to fit into the office
  • Outdated tech in use
  • Lack of engagement in meetings

Three cases, as written

Company saving M&A falls through

A struggling company has spent months on a merger hailed internally as the deal of the decade. At the eleventh hour it collapses over valuation and integration. Investors pull out, employees fear layoffs and competitors circle.

Precedent: Microsoft offered $44.6 billion for Yahoo in 2008, Yahoo said no, and Yahoo was later sold to Verizon for a fraction of that offer.

The team has to handle lost market confidence, internal turmoil, a competitor advantage, an unclear strategy and a tight budget, all at once.

Death of the CEO or co founder with an IPO imminent

A billion dollar AI healthcare company is weeks from its IPO when its founder dies. There is no successor, investors are jittery and power struggles start inside the executive team.

Precedent: Apple after Steve Jobs in 2011.

The team has to decide whether to delay the IPO, who leads in the meantime, and how to keep staff, investors and the press steady.

Payment page breakdown

An e-commerce platform's payment page starts failing just before the holiday season. Support is swamped and social media is turning on the brand.

Precedent: several major retailers hit payment failures on Black Friday in 2020.

The team has to find the fault, tell customers what is happening, stand up a backup way to pay, and make sure it cannot happen again.

Scope

The companies inside each scenario are fictional. The precedents are real and are there to ground the discussion, not to judge it.
